# Standards
il mondo della mobilità offre diversi standard per divulgare i dati di TPL e di BikeSharing.
I formati più noti sono GTFS e GBFS

## GTFS
formato deciso da Google per la piattaforma Google Transit ora integrata in Google Maps attraverso cui si può svolgere la pianificazione multimodale e viene utilizzato per definire gli orari degli autobus
### documentazione ufficiale
- https://developers.google.com/transit/gtfs/

### tools
esistono varie librerie e applicazioni che permettono di gestire i GTFS
- OpenTripPlanner
- [UrbanAccess](https://udst.github.io/urbanaccess/index.html)


## GBFS
analogo a GTFS ma studiato per il Bike Sharing ed ora adattato anche per i monopattini elettrici.
### documentazione ufficiale
- https://github.com/NABSA/gbfs

### tools
- https://bikeitaly.github.io/gbfs-viewer/


## [Mobility Data Specification - Open Mobility Foundation](https://github.com/openmobilityfoundation/mobility-data-specification)

The Mobility Data Specification [(MDS)](https://www.openmobilityfoundation.org/about-mds/#:~:text=time%20and%20money.-,MDS%E2%80%94%E2%80%9CMobility%20Data%20Specification%E2%80%9D%E2%80%94is%20a%20digital%20tool,scooter%20and%20bike%2Dshare%20companies.) defined by the Open Mobility Foundation (OMF) is a framework for standardizing and sharing mobility data, helping operators and cities to exchange information in a standard format. MDS has six distinct APIs: the main one are the *provider*, the *agency*,  and the *policy*, while other APIs are the *geography*, the *jurisdiction*, and the *metrics*.

*Provider* and *agency* express a common language about how data should be shaped, while the *policy* API is a common language for rules and regulations, enabling cities to set rules that operators have to follow. 

The *policy* allows cities to establish options to ensure distribution of vehicles and coverage of specific areas. This is done using the GeoJSON standard. To safeguard privacy, *policy* does not require real-time service.

The *provider* is the most used and it is designed to be implemented by operators, which are heavily involved in MDS creation and testing. The provider API enables regulators to query historical information about trips and vehicles status.

The *agency* API is designed for regulatory agencies to capture specific events, such as trips starts, and allows for monitoring of the mobility service in real-time. For this reason, there are some privacy concerns, making this API the least implemented.

The *geography* API endpoints are implemented by regulatory agency and used by mobility providers in order to get information about geographical regions. This API was originally included in the *policy* one.

The *jurisdiction* API endpoints are implemented by regulatory agency and are meant to allow cities to communicate boundaries between one another and to mobility providers. 

The *metrics* API endpoints are implemented by regulatory agency or their representatives to obtain a standard way to describe available metrics.

Even though MDS is inspired by standards like GTFS (General Transit Feed Specification) and GBFS (General Bikeshare Feed Specification), there are some differences between GBFS and MDS, the main one being that vehicles IDs are not static in public GBFS, which makes it difficult to track individual vehicles but also alleviates privacy concerns.

MDS is a widely use standard for pursuing, among other purposes, the determination of the total number of vehicles per operator, to ensure vehicles are distributed according to equity requirements, to investigate injuries and collisions with other objects and cars and determine causes, to find locations where vehicles are operating or passing, to investigate complaints from residents, and for infrastructure and urban planning.

MDS data is generated by vehicles, and includes information about their status, location, and routes, avoiding any personal information in order to protect privacy. Personal data, such as name, contacts, and payment information, are collected only by the operators offering the service, but never included in MDS. Data describing the status of vehicles passes from the company operating the service to the city regulating, never passing through the rider.
